Foundation of Safety

The 10 Golden Rules of Power Distribution Safety

Universally recognized principles adopted from OSHA 1910.269, NFPA 70E, and IEC 61936. Non-negotiable for every GEPCO officer.

01

Isolate & Lockout/Tagout

Never work on energized equipment without verified isolation. Apply LOTO, test for zero energy, and prove the absence of voltage before touching any conductor.

OSHA 1910.147
02

Test Before You Touch

Treat every line as live until personally tested and grounded. Use calibrated voltage detectors and insulating gloves rated for the voltage class.

IEEE 516
03

Correct PPE, Every Time

Wear arc-rated clothing, insulated gloves, hard hats, safety boots, and eye protection appropriate to the hazard level. No shortcuts, no exceptions.

NFPA 70E
04

Maintain Safe Approach Distances

Respect minimum approach distances (MAD) for all voltage classes. Never breach the restricted zone without proper insulated tools and authorization.

OSHA 1910.269
05

Permit to Work & Toolbox Talks

Every job begins with a Permit to Work (PTW), Job Safety Analysis (JSA), and a documented toolbox talk. No briefing, no work.

ISO 45001
06

Stop Work Authority

Every officer has the unconditional right and duty to stop work when conditions become unsafe — without fear of retaliation.

Behavior-Based Safety
07

Working at Heights

Full-body harness, 100% fall-arrest tie-off, and inspected ladders and buckets. Pole-top rescue equipment must be on-site before ascending.

ANSI Z359
08

Arc Flash & Blast Protection

Calculate incident energy, respect arc flash boundaries, and wear arc-rated PPE matching the incident energy level. Never turn your back on a live panel.

NFPA 70E / IEEE 1584
09

Report Every Incident

Near misses, injuries, property damage — report within 15 minutes. Transparency saves lives. Concealment costs them.

OSHA Recordkeeping
10

Go Home Safe, Every Day

Your family expects you home. Your team depends on you. Fatigue, distraction, and complacency are hazards — manage them like you manage voltage.

Risk Mitigation Framework

Hierarchy of Hazard Controls

Always eliminate first — don't rely on PPE as your only defense. This is the ISO 45001 and OSHA-recommended approach.

1

Elimination

Physically remove the hazard — e.g., de-energize the line before working on it.

Most Effective
2

Substitution

Replace the hazard with something safer — e.g., use insulated tools instead of bare-hand contact.

Highly Effective
3

Engineering Controls

Isolate people from the hazard — e.g., barriers, insulating blankets, arc-rated enclosures.

Effective
4

Administrative Controls

Change the way people work — e.g., permits, procedures, training, rotations.

Moderate
5

PPE

Protect the worker with personal equipment — the last line of defense, never the first.

Least Effective

Critical Response

Emergency Response Timeline

Every second matters. Follow this sequence exactly during any electrical incident.

T+0

🚨 Secure the Scene

Stop all work. Warn others. Do NOT approach the victim if the conductor may still be energized. Maintain safe distance and call for backup.

T+30s

⚡ De-Energize the Source

Notify the control room immediately. Trip the breaker / open the isolator. Confirm zero energy with a voltage detector before any rescue attempt.

T+1m

📞 Activate Emergency Services

Call 1122 (Rescue) and GEPCO Emergency Line. Provide exact location, number of casualties, and voltage involved. Stay on the line.

T+2m

🩹 Provide First Aid & CPR

Only after de-energization. Begin CPR if trained. Treat burns with clean, dry dressing. Do not apply ointments or remove stuck clothing.

T+15m

📝 Report & Preserve Evidence

Notify the Safety Officer. Photograph the scene. Preserve the equipment. Document witness statements. Submit the incident report within 24 hours.

Continuous Learning

Safety Training Modules for Officers

Structured competency development aligned with international certification pathways.

⚡ Module 1: Electrical Hazard Awareness

+

Foundational training on the four primary electrical hazards: shock, arc flash, arc blast, and thermal burns. Officers learn how current flows through the body, the difference between contact and step potential, and how to recognize hazardous conditions before they escalate.

  • Ohm's Law applied to body impedance
  • Incident energy calculation (IEEE 1584)
  • Arc flash boundary determination
  • Case studies of real distribution incidents

🔒 Module 2: Lockout / Tagout (LOTO) Mastery

+

Hands-on certification on the 6-step LOTO process: Prepare, Notify, Shutdown, Isolate, Lock & Tag, Verify. Includes group lockout procedures for multi-crew jobs and tag removal protocols.

  • Personal lock assignment and control
  • Try-out / test for absence of voltage
  • Stored energy release (capacitors, springs, hydraulic)
  • Shift-change lockout transfer procedures

🪜 Module 3: Working at Heights & Pole-Top Rescue

+

Certification in fall arrest systems, 100% tie-off discipline, and pole-top rescue techniques. Meets ANSI Z359 and OSHA 1910.269 requirements.

  • Harness inspection & fitting
  • Ladder and bucket truck safety
  • Rescue of a suspended worker (self-rescue & assisted)
  • Anchor point selection and load ratings

🧯 Module 4: Arc Flash & PPE Selection

+

NFPA 70E-based training on selecting arc-rated clothing, calculating incident energy, and understanding PPE categories. Officers perform arc flash risk assessments on real switchgear.

  • Arc-rated vs. flame-resistant clothing
  • PPE category table and selection
  • Rubber insulating glove classes & testing
  • Arc flash labeling and boundaries

🚑 Module 5: First Aid, CPR & Electrical Rescue

+

Certified first-aid and CPR training focused on electrical injuries: burns, cardiac arrest, and neurological effects. Includes safe rescue from energized equipment.

  • Safe removal of victim from contact
  • CPR and AED operation
  • Burn treatment and shock management
  • Emergency communication protocols

🧠 Module 6: Behavior-Based Safety & Leadership

+

Designed for officers leading crews. Focuses on safety leadership, observation, coaching, and building a positive safety culture where workers feel empowered to speak up.

  • Conducting safety observations
  • Positive reinforcement techniques
  • Handling pushback and complacency
  • Leading safety meetings effectively
